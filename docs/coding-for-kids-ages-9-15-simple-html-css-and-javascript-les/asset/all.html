Introduction

 

It was a bright summer afternoon in Atlanta; and I grabbed my laptop to watch the latest episode of ‘Game of Thrones’ on the porch outside. As I sat on the three-legged chair; I felt a wobble on the rear leg. The leg just came off the stool and I crashed to the ground on my back. Hearing the loud thud on the porch, both my wife, Isabelle, and son, Arnad, came out. “Are you ok?” Isabelle gasped. I was shaken, but fine, as Isabelle helped me up. I walked inside and grabbed a glass of water to get me back to my senses. To my amazement, I noticed, through the window, my 9-year-old son, Arnad try to figure out how to piece it together. He assembled the leg back in and noticed that the chair was still wobbling. He then switched out the screw with the one on the good leg; and the chair was still wobbling. He finally glued the sliding mechanism that held the screw in place; and that magically fixed the chair. Wow!!

Next, we can talk about Betty. Our neighbour’s daughter, Betty, struggled with Social Sciences for the longest time. Her parents were used to failing grades in this topic; and were relieved each time she got a ‘D’. So, imagine their shock when Betty came back one day with a report card that showed an ‘A’ in social science. Yeah, Betty was a hardworking girl and she did do well in some of her subjects; but this turnaround was unexpected. Then, Betty showed her parents her social science improvement plan in the last three months. She broke down the list of topics she would work on every week; and how she would simplify some of the harder topics with documentaries and group study sessions. She systematically moved on from topic to topic till she addressed all of them.

And finally, we address Carl. Carl was a tennis player at the start of high school. He was a lot smaller and weaker than his opponents. As a result, he lost pretty badly in his first tournament. He struggled to even get a few points. While he was upset, he realized that his loss was not a loss due to lack of skill; it was based of circumstances. He analysed the situation; and realized that he needed to improve his skill and positional play on the court. He analysed the movement of his opponents on the court; and adapted his shots to always make sure they played him off balance. He did plenty of yoga and other stretching exercises to improve his flexibility, balance and speed. He started improving month after month. Every month, he practiced a different shot. In a couple of years, he grew into his frame and was much stronger and wider. And due to his hard work, he was much more skilled than his opponents. In his third year of high school, he won the annual tournament with really no competition at all.

All of these situations are hypothetical and can happen due to innate characteristics of certain kids. Also, coding does not guarantee that you will learn these skills. But, all of these situations demonstrate skills that can be learnt during coding. Arnad systematically examined the chair and its different components; and worked through each component to find the problem. This is the same as a process called ‘debugging’ in programming. Debugging is what happens when a program doesn’t work. It requires systematic analysis of the code to find the problem and make it work.

Betty looked at a problem; and organized it into different parts that are required to achieve a result. This is similar to problem solving in programming. This is understanding what is required to solve a coding problem; and structure the code into different sections to make the program work.

Carl is doing the same thing as Betty; he is structuring a problem into different parts so it can be solved in a simple manner. But, in addition, he is prioritizing different skills on the tennis court. He spent time figuring out which skills are more important to beating his opponent and worked on those first. Prioritization is an important skill in programming after a code is properly structured. Coders want to prioritize more vital tasks in the code to test first.

All these skills are vital to programming; and your kids should learn these if they learn how to code well. It doesn’t really matter if they become programmers. The skills will benefit your kids for life. Programming also helps kids improve at basic math and reading; in addition to better logic and problem-solving skills.

These skills, combined with communication skills they learn in high school, are the reason why software engineers are paid a median annual salary of $112000 (as of 2018) in America. This is over double the average salary in America.

Despite this, high schools all over the world, even in affluent areas, lack the staff and technical resources to handle this task. According to a 2000 K-12 teacher survey done by Tech Republic, only 8% of teachers felt reasonably confident to teach children programming. 50% of kids in America do not have access to the internet at home. The statistics are even worse for girls and children of color in America; who have less resources and role models to look up to.

So, you need to take to take this into your own hands.

What Not To Do

You might be wondering what you need to do to get started. There’s a couple of things I advise against doing. I would advise against hiring a programmer who has no experience in teaching kids. They are experts in the topic; but it’s been decades since they started programming; so, they can’t relate to a kid who hasn’t learnt programming before and who’s learning basic Mathematics. The next thing to avoid is to search for the latest textbook on JavaScript; and read it out for your kid. These are as useful for learning programming; as a dictionary is for learning a language. They are expensive, and more useful as a reference tool for existing programmers. I also recommend against coding camps. These can work for a minority of students; but most students get lost in the large group of kids that are learning. One to one attention is the best way to get going.

Intro to html

 

What is html?

Html stands for Hyper Test Markup Language

Html is the language that humans use to connect with web browsers like Chrome, Internet Explorer, Firefox etc.

Sometimes, it is assisted by css and JavaScript; but the basic structure of a web page is defined by html.

It is pretty simple and easy to grasp for even beginners to programming and young kids.

Html is done using simple tags that look like the ends of an arrow.

Example of a html tag

Here’s what a html tag looks like:

<p> - Example of a tag

Every html tag has an extra tag that signals the end of the tag. The ending of a tag has a slash in it in addition to the two ends of a tag. See below for a simple example.

 

<p> - Tag starts

</p> - Tag ends

Basic html tags and What They’re Used For

 

<html>

This is the tag that starts every piece of html code.

 

<head>

This is the code that is executed first before anything else.

 

<title>

This is the code written in the top ribbon of the browser.

 

<body>

Includes the information that appears on the page.

 

<p>

This is used for a paragraph of text that appears on the body.

 

<h1>, <h2>, <h3>, <h4>, <h5>, <h6>

These are used for headings on text in descending order of size. <h1> is the largest size heading, and <h6> is the smallest.

 

<strong>

This is used to bold text

 

<ul>

This is used for unordered lists such as bulleted items

 

<li>

Used to add a bullet icon before a line of text

 

<ol>

This is used for unordered lists such as items number from 1,2,3…

 

<div>

These are larger sections of content; that generally contain several paragraphs, headings, etc.

 

<a>

This is used to hyperlink text. Hyperlink is what happens when you click on a piece of underlined text and it links to another page

 

<img>

This is used for adding images to a web page.

JavaScript Example

 

What is JavaScript?

JavaScript is a high-level programming language. It is most commonly used to perform user actions on a html page.

It also helps to enhance interactions of users on a web page.

How to Use JavaScript in html

There are two ways to use JavaScript on a html page:

The first way is to just include JavaScript code within <script> tags. It would be like below:

 

<html>

<head>

<title> </title>

</head>

<body>

<script>

</script>

</body>

</html>

 

The JavaScript code would be within the two script tags shown in red above.

 

The second way is to create a Javascript file and save it as a .js file. Then you would reference the Javascript file in the html code. This is shown below.

 

 

<html>

<head>

<title> </title>

</head>

<body>

<script src="newfile.js"></script>

</body>

</html>

 

You would include all the JavaScript code in the newfile.js file.

 

JavaScript Terms You Need to Know

 

We are going through a couple of JavaScript html code examples next. We need to know the following terms to understand the code.

 

function

A JavaScript function is any set of lines of code that performs a task. A function is normally made to execute tasks that need to happen when you click on a html button. Every function has a name and is referenced in the html code. An example is below:

function abcd()

abcd is the name of the function that performs a specific task. It will be referenced in html code as abcd.

 

 

Variable

A variable is used to store names or numbers in JavaScript. Think of it as a box that can store one value. That box is referenced each time you need that value. It is defined as below:

var flag=1;

flag is the name of the variable and it stores the value of 1. So anytime flag is referenced in the JavaScript program; it has a value of 1 (unless you change the value of flag). This value of flag can be used to define other variables. For example:

var king=flag*2;

king now has a value of 1*2=2. So variables are a good way of dealing with unknown quantities.

 

document.getElementbyID().value

This javascript term is used to get the value of an item in a html form textbox. So, if you fill out a form textbox with your name and click on a button, the document.getElementbyID() gets the value of what you typed in the box. As an example:

var yname=document.getElementById("name").value;

“name” is the id of the form textbox that is defined in html. To see how this is done in html, check out the section on html tags in the previous chapter.

Whatever is typed in the textbox is stored in a variable called yname. It will be referenced in javascript from now on as yname.

 

Document.write()

This function is used to print anything on the web browser.

For example,

document.write(“xyz”);

Prints xyz on the screen

You can also add two different terms by using the “+” button.

document.write(“xyz”+”123”);

Prints xyz123 on the screen.

This is useful when you want to add text to a variable. For example.

var yname=document.getElementById("name").value;

document.write(“My name is “+yname);

If you typed in “Matt” in the textbox, it prints out “My name is Matt” on the screen.

 

parseint()

parseint changes the number to a whole number, whether it is a decimal or not. For example,

parseint(2.8) would equal 3.

Parseint(2) would just be 2.

 

 

parsefloat()

parsefloat() does the opposite of parseint(). It changes the number to decimal. For example,

parseint(2) would equal 2.0

Parseint(3.1) would just be 3.1

Conditional Statement

What is the conditional statement?

It is a statement that checks if a certain condition is true. It can be as simple as checking if a number is less than 10 to something as checking medical samples from patients for certain medical conditions.

In Javascript, the conditional statement is done using IF ELSE statements.

So, it basically checks that if something is true, then perform an action. If it’s not true, then do something else. As an example,

IF (George==”Hungry”)

{

Document.write(‘Give George a Snack);

}

ELSE

{

Document.write(‘No snack for George’);

}

In the above example, the code checks if George is hungry and performs one of two actions. If George is hungry then it prints ‘Give George a snack’. If not, then print ‘No snack for George’)

This is a simple example of a conditional in JavaScript using IF ELSE statement.

Nested Conditional Statements

 

These are basically conditional statements within conditional statements. These are also called nested loops.

If he’s not hungry, then print ‘No snack for George’).

This is a simple example of a nested IF ELSE statement in JavaScript.

Arrays

What is An Array?

An array is basically a group of items that are stored in a single name. They are accessed using different indexes of the name. For example, in Javascript:

var name[5] is an array of size 5. The first element is name[0], the second element is name[1] and so on…..

So, if we want to assign values to the array:

var name[5]=[“Abhi”,”Bob”,”Aaron”,”Steve”,”Mark”]

The above creates an array of size 5. To print the first element in JavaScript, we do:

document.write(name[0]);

This prints out Abhi, which is the first element of the array.

If we do:

document.write(name[4]);

This prints out Mark, which is the last element of the array.

If we want to add an element to the array use the push function as below:

name.push(“Carl”);

document.write(name[5]);

This prints out Carl, which is the new element of the array.

Math Functions

This is amazing so far. But what if you want to make a mini calculator? Or teach math to kids? Learning how to code Math functions is a great way to also increase the pace at which your kids understand Math. You can set up Math tests for your kids and verify them with JavaScript functions.

Let’s get started with some basic Math functions that we can implement in JavaScript.

Math.Round()

Math.Round() rounds a decimal number to the nearest whole number. For example, Math.Round(1.635) rounds to 2; while Math.Round(1.43) rounds to 1

Math.ceil()

Math.ceil() round to the higher number. It basically rounds up to the higher whole number. For example, Math.ceil(1.67) returns 2; while Math.ceil(2.01) returns 3.

Math.floor()

Math.floor() rounds to the lower whole number. It basically removes the decimal points from a decimal number. So, Math.floor(1.47) returns 1; and Math.floor(2.34) also returns 2.

Math.Sqrt()

Math.Sqrt() returns the square root of a number. So, Math.sqrt(9) returns 3; while Math.sqrt(16) returns 4.

Math.min()

Math.min() returns the minimum value of an array. Just put the array inside the bracket. So, if

a=[13,11,34,54,9];

Math.min(A) returns 9

Math.max()

Math.min() returns the maximum value of an array. So for the above example, Math.max(A) returns 54.

 

Math.Random()

Math.Random() is a cool function. It returns a random decimal number between 0 and 1; not including 1.

So, Math.Random() could include 0.95 the first time and 0.43 the second time.

So, you could play a guess the number game between 0 and 10. Just do this below:

Math.Round(Math.Random()*10) gives you a random whole number between 0 and 10.
