There is computer hardware made for specific purposes like performing calculations, etc. and then there are general purpose computers that can be re-purposed to do different tasks by anyone, for example our laptops, desktops, etc. However the first time the term “Computer” appeared it was used to describe a job title of actual people who were paid to perform computations.![ the Ishango bone](Image00005.jpg)​ The first computing devices are were invented when people set out to solve problems that surpassed the computing ability of their minds. There is history of Computing devices that goes back thousands of years. The earliest recorded computing devices are tally sticks like the Ishango bone.​ These tally sticks were used mostly for mnemonic purposes and basic but lengthy calculations. The Ishango bone was used around 19,000 BC which was the Old Stone Age. A relatively-modern example of this type of technology can be imagined as prayer beads, and electronic counters.![ part of Antikythera mechanism](Image00006.jpg)​ There are are records of Ancient computing devices like the Antikythera mechanism which was used to predict astronomical positions. In the picture we can see a part of this device. It was discovered by Greek archaeologist Valerios Stais in 1901 among the wreckage retrieved near Antikythera island.​ The instrument is believed to have been designed by Greek scientists, and is acknowledged as the first known analogue computer. Its complexity suggests that it must have predecessors that built up to this. It is determined to be made around 87 BC.​ However when it comes to ancient computers we can’t forget the well known Chinese abacus aka “The Suanpan”. First appeared in a 190 CE book of Eastern Han Dynasty called “Supplementary Notes on the Art of Figures” by Xu Yue”.While the exact design of this device is not known, this device could be used to perform both decimal and hexadecimal computations.![ Abacus ](Image00007.jpg)​ Different versions of the Abacus were used around the world, the most famous versions being the Suanpan, and the Roman hand abacus.![The Pascaline aka Pascal’s calculator ](Image00008.jpg)​ Then there was The Pascaline aka Pascal’s calculator invented by French Mathematician Blaise Pascal.​ This machine was able to perform addition of two numbers, and multiplication, division through repeated addition and subtraction.​ The Pascaline was famous for its ability to carry leftover to next units.​ This machine was designed in 1642.​ Later in 1672 German mathematician Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz famously known as the sort of a co-inventor of calculus developed a device called “The Stepped Reckoner”.![](Image00009.jpg)​ In this image we can see the replace of the Stepped Reckoner. This was the first calculator that could perform all the arithmetic calculations. The machine was beyond the technology of its time and had mechanical problems in addition to a significant design flaw that rendered it unable to perform reliably.![ the Arithmomètre](Image00010.jpg)​ Then in 1851 came the Arithmomètre, the first digital mechanical calculator strong and reliable enough to be used offices.​ This calculator could add and subtract two numbers directly and could perform long multiplications and divisions effectively.​ A man named Charles Xavier Thomas de Colmar patented and manufactured this design from 1851 to 1915.​ It was the first commercially successful mechanical calculator.​ With the launch of the Arithmomètre the mechanical calculator industry came to be; and for forty years, from 1851 to 1890, the Arithmomètre was the only type of mechanical calculator in commercial production.![](Image00011.jpg)​ Then came the revolutionary “Punch card” era. From the invention of computer programming languages up to the mid-1970s, most computer programmers created, edited and stored their programs/software on punched cards. A typing error resulted in throwing away the punch card and redoing all the work again.​ The punch cards were a digital mechanical technology, like all mechanical tech it was subject to wear and tear. This technology was applied on tape, paper, wood, metal sheets, etc. and was very successful. The technology is so practical that it is used even today in many different devices, mostly antique replicas and factories.​ However here is the important stuff, The punch card was probably the first medium to store digital data for Electronic computation purposes. Punch cards were used in looms, calculators, and musical instruments like those piano’s with built-in scrolls that can play music without a player, you know the ones we saw on “Tom and Jerry”.![The Difference Engine](Image00012.jpg)​ In 1819 an English Mathematician named Charles Babbage started the construction of a device called “The Difference Engine”, which was a device made to work with polynomial functions (remember algebra I).​ The device was completed in 1822, and Babbage made his invention public on the 14th of June, 1822.​ Believe it or not but the Difference Engine was in fact closely related to the Antikythera mechanism in a manner of speaking.​ Then in 1837 Charles Babbage proposed a successor to this device and called it “The Analytical Engine”. It was a mechanical general-purpose computer, like the modern computers we use today. It had an ALU aka arithmetic logic unit, a control flow in the form of conditional branching and loops, and integrated memory, in other words it was in fact an ancestor of the modern computer.​ After all this went down with Babbage a string of other devices came to the scene, like the following ones:![Mallock Machine](Image00013.jpg)​ There was the “Mallock Machine”, built by Rawlyn Richard Manconchy Mallock of Cambridge University in 1933\. It was an electrical analogue computer used to solve simultaneous linear differential equations.​ Then there was the “Water Integrator” built in the soviet union in 1936 by Vladimir Lukyanov. It could solve (partial) differential equations. It have chambers where water levels represented stored numbers, and the rate of flow between them represented mathematical operations.![Model K](Image00014.jpg)​ There was also the “Model K”, built in 1936 a scientist named George Robert Stibitz from Bell Labs. It was the first electric digital computer.​ There was the “Z1” computer, a mechanical device designed and built by German scientist Konrad Zuse around 1936 to 1938\. It was a binary electrically driven mechanical calculator with limited programmability.​ Then from 1930’s to 1940’s there was a wave of computers called “The Differential Analysers”, they were basically mechanical analogue computers designed to solve differential equations by integration, using wheel-and-disc mechanisms to perform the integration.​ In WWII an English telephone engineer Thomas Harold Flowers from the British Post Office designed and built the “Colossus” computer at Bletchley Park park; the world's first programmable electronic computer. It’s purpose was to help solve encrypted German messages. A similar thing was done earlier at Bletchley Park by Alan Turing when he created an electro-mechanical device to rival the Nazi Enigma Machine.​ Alan Turing is widely considered the father of theoretical computer science, and AI. Much of Turing’s work was either classified or deep-sixed by the British government under the Official Secrets Act.![Alan Turing](Image00015.jpg)​ ENIAC was the first electronic general-purpose computer, designed by John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert for military purposes in 1945.![](Image00016.jpg)​ The Manchester Baby aka The Small-Scale Experimental Machine (SSEM) was the world's first electronic computer that could store its programs inside electronic memory. *   Renderer Process : It’s usually referring to files that are attached to our HTML files, like renderer.js in the electron-quick-start approach.
*   Preload File: This is a Javascript file that is always loaded with every web-page by default, without mentioning it in their HTML file.
